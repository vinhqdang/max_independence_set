Computational Optimization and Applications

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Exact Large-Neighbourhood Search for the
Maximum Independent Set Problem on Massive Sparse Graphs", for publication in
Computational Optimization and Applications. It is a computational study of a
local-search method for a core combinatorial optimization problem, with an algorithm,
a proof of admissibility and monotonicity of its move, an implementation and an
experimental evaluation.

WHAT THE PAPER DOES

The maximum independent set problem (equivalently minimum vertex cover) is usually
attacked on large sparse graphs by safe reduction rules followed by local search. The
paper studies a region move for that search: release a set of incumbent vertices,
collect the outside vertices whose incumbent neighbours all lie in the set, and re-solve
the resulting region by branch-and-reduce. Nothing outside the region conflicts with it,
so the incumbent never worsens, including when sub-solves are truncated by a budget.

WHAT IS AND IS NOT NEW

The region construction is that of Borowitz, Grossmann and Schulz (ALENEX 2025) for
dynamic independent sets, and the manuscript says so. Its contribution is the use of
that construction as the inner move of a static solver, with reductions applied inside
each region and an online rule for the number of released vertices, together with an
experimental study that includes negative results. On 27 instances in five families at
a 60-second budget the method matches or exceeds the best of six established solvers on
16 and is strictly best on three Delaunay triangulations; it is not significantly
different from ReduMIS, NuMVC or FastVC under a Holm-corrected Wilcoxon test on relative
gaps. Ablations separate exact repair from reductions inside the region, and kernel
measurements show that residual kernel size alone does not predict where the method
helps. The comparison solvers date from 2012-2019, and the manuscript states that more
recent solvers (for example InfVC) were not run.

PRIOR REVIEW AND TRANSPARENCY

An earlier version was reviewed by another journal and not accepted, and a later version
was declined by a second journal on grounds of scope without scientific assessment. The

Every solution that a solver emits is independently verified. Source code, instance
