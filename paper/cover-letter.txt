Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Exact Large-Neighbourhood Search for
the Maximum Independent Set Problem on Massive Sparse Graphs", for publication
as a full-length research paper in Computers & Operations Research. I believe it
falls under the journal's area of Optimization -- Approximate Methods.

THE PROBLEM

The maximum independent set problem, equivalently minimum vertex cover, is the
model for selecting a largest mutually compatible subset when incompatibility is
pairwise. The conflict graphs that arise from real data -- in scheduling,
interference-limited wireless assignment, map labelling and record
de-duplication -- routinely have millions of vertices and average degree in the
low tens. For a decade the practical approach to such instances has been to
apply safe data reduction rules until none fires and then search whatever
survives. Methods differ almost entirely in that second step, and they share a
weakness: instances whose reduced graph, or kernel, remains large.

THE CONTRIBUTION

The paper studies a neighbourhood that is solved exactly rather than sampled.
Releasing a set F of vertices from an incumbent solution makes eligible exactly
those vertices all of whose incumbent neighbours lie in F. The paper proves that
the resulting region is separated from the remainder of the incumbent, so the
region may be re-solved without any consistency check against the rest of the
solution; that acceptance is safe whenever the returned region solution is no
smaller than the set released, which is what preserves monotonicity when the
sub-solver is truncated; and that maximality is preserved outside the region, so
repair is confined to the region and its neighbourhood rather than the graph.

Two things make this affordable in an inner loop. The reduction rules, normally
used once as preprocessing, are applied inside each move, collapsing a region of
thousands of vertices to a kernel of a few dozen. The size of F is not a
parameter but is inferred at run time from whether the sub-problems are reaching
proven optimality, which is what allows a single configuration to cover instance
families whose appropriate neighbourhood sizes differ by three orders of
magnitude.

The paper also reports the engineering that the throughput rests on -- a dynamic
graph with payload-free LIFO undo over a twin-indexed adjacency array, sub-solver
reuse across moves, and a degree-ordered reduction queue -- with measurements
rather than assertions, since the method's viability is a claim about constant
factors as much as about the neighbourhood.

THE EVIDENCE, AND WHAT IT DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW

The computational study covers 27 instances in five families against six
baselines under a common 60-second budget, with a ten-fold budget study, a seed
study, a parameter-sensitivity study, an ablation, and a replication on
different hardware.

I have tried to report the outcome without inflating it. The method attains the
best value of all seven solvers on every geometric instance, and is strictly
ahead of all of them on the three Delaunay instances. Over the benchmark as a
whole it is strictly ahead on three instances, matches the best value on
thirteen, and is behind on eleven; a paired Wilcoxon test finds no significant
difference from the strongest baseline. The ablation attributes the geometric
margin to the exact neighbourhood move, and reports three further components
that contributed nothing, including a move design that the measurements showed
to be worthless. A section on threats to validity states where the method does
not help and why the largest single margin is in part a difference in
convergence rate rather than in final quality.

CONFORMANCE

The heuristic is described in metaphor-free language throughout. The paper
reports algorithmic cost bounds for the move and its construction alongside the
numerical experiments. The source code, the instance generators and the raw
per-run records behind every table and figure are publicly available, and the
appendix documents instance provenance in the detail needed to rebuild the
benchmark exactly -- including a normalisation issue in a widely used benchmark
family whose two distributed forms are easily confused, which we believe is of
independent use to others reporting on it.

The work is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration for publication elsewhere. I am the sole author and there are no
competing interests to declare.
